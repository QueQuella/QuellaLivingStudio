IDEA PRESENTATION · 2026

PROGRAMMING DREAM

An Interactive World Between Human Imagination and Machine Interpretation

6 Slides · 5–6 Minutes

01 / THE IDEA

WHAT IF A DRAWING COULD COME ALIVE?

PROGRAMMING DREAM
Drawing Life into a Digital Dream

Programming Dream is a 3D exploration game. Players explore an endless ocean, draw abstract patterns, and watch their drawings become digital creatures through AI interpretation.

EXPLORE → DRAW → INTERPRET → BRING TO LIFE

02 / MOTIVATION

CAN AI BECOME A CREATIVE PARTNER?

I want to explore whether AI can become a creative partner by responding to what a player draws. Learning about deep learning and CNNs made me curious about how a machine sees a pattern, and how an unexpected interpretation might open up a new idea. My central interests are human–AI collaboration, machine perception, and creative uncertainty. Explore neural networks in TensorFlow Playground ↗

03 / INSPIRATION

ARTIFICIAL LIFE & LIVING VIRTUAL WORLDS

My project is inspired by interactive artworks that explore artificial life and the relationship between humans and digital organisms. Both references demonstrate how computational systems can transform virtual creatures from simple visual objects into living, responsive experiences.

01 — EMISSARIES

Ian Cheng (2015–2017)
Live Simulation / Game Engine Art

A series of self-running simulations in which virtual characters interact within unpredictable digital ecosystems. It inspires me to create a virtual environment where creatures can exist and behave independently.

View Artwork ↗

02 — A-VOLVE

Christa Sommerer & Laurent Mignonneau (1994)
Interactive Installation / Artificial Life

Visitors draw shapes on a touchscreen to create virtual organisms that swim and interact in a water environment. It directly inspires my drawing-to-creature interaction and the idea of giving digital forms a sense of life.

View Artwork ↗

My Direction: I refer to Emissaries for its independently behaving creatures and A-Volve for its drawing-based participation. These references guide my own exploration of machine perception in a dreamlike ocean. The Museum of Modern Art

04 / PATHWAY & PLAN

FROM DRAWING TO DIGITAL LIFE

SELECTED PATHWAY — GAME ENGINE & INTERACTIVE MEDIA

I will develop a small interactive prototype in UE. The player enters a dreamlike ocean, approaches a floating computer, and draws an abstract pattern. UE captures the drawing as an image and passes its resized, normalised pixel data to the trained model. A trained CNN interprets the drawing and identifies a creature type. After confirmation, the corresponding 3D creature appears in the ocean, where the player can observe it and continue exploring.

05 / TOOLS & AI

CONNECTING ART, AI & GAME DESIGN

UE will capture the player's drawing as an image and resize and normalise its pixels to match the training data. An inference plugin or C++ bridge will feed this data into the trained .onnx model and convert its output scores into a creature label. Blueprints will map the returned label to the matching creature Blueprint and use Spawn Actor from Class to create it after the player confirms.

PRODUCTION WORKFLOW

01 — 3D DESIGN

Blender + UE

Environment, creatures, lighting, and animation.

02 — AI TRAINING

Python + PyTorch + JupyterLab

Drawing dataset, CNN training, and data augmentation.

03 — INTERACTIVE SYSTEM

UE Blueprints + Model Inference

Drawing interface, prediction results, and creature spawning.

ROLE OF AI

AI will help me write and debug code, generate extra training patterns, and develop modelling references and rough assets. I will also use it to work through CNN training, model integration, and Blueprint logic.

06 / QUESTIONS & CHALLENGES

WHEN DOES AI BECOME MORE THAN A TOOL?

Moving a drawing from UE to the deep learning model may take processing time, and resizing or changing its pixel values may lose details that affect recognition accuracy. I need to test how well the model reads different drawing styles and decide on clear drawing rules, such as canvas size, stroke thickness, and simple shape hints. These rules should help the machine read the patterns while leaving players room to express their own ideas.

I also want to study how rough textures and simple low-poly shapes express digital nostalgia, anti-fidelity aesthetics, and imperfection. Thinking through art modding and cargo cult coding, I see these figures as constructed digital objects, shaped by a bricoleur who reworks existing tools and fragments. Incomplete visual clues may invite players to use memory, experience, and visual schemas to form guesses and fill in the gaps. This may encourage imagination, but the link between visual uncertainty and imaginative engagement remains an open question for this project, and responses may differ between players. I could explore this through saved drawings and player choices, alongside brief player feedback about what they imagined.

THE OPEN QUESTION

Can the difference between what we imagine and what AI understands become a new form of creative expression?

Programming Dream explores a world where human imagination begins the process, machine interpretation shapes the result, and digital life emerges.

03 / ABOUT THE PROJECTIN DEVELOPMENT

Programming Dream✦

Between human imagination
and machine interpretation.

Programming Dream is a small interactive art experiment set in a dreamlike digital ocean. A floating computer invites players to draw abstract patterns, which a trained neural network interprets as one of six low-poly creatures. These handmade creatures emerge into the sea, turning each drawing into an encounter between what a person imagines and what a machine recognises. Through rough textures, uncertain forms, and moments of surprise, I explore how digital life can leave room for our own imagination.
